\title{Spectrum-Matched Quantum-Assisted PINNs:\\
  A Constructive Design Methodology for Problem-Specific\\
  Variational Circuits, Instrumented by Explainable-AI Diagnostics}
\author{Katta Lahari \and Mannava Daasaradhi}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) learn low frequencies fast and high
frequencies slowly (spectral bias), because their neural tangent kernel (NTK) spectrum
decays with frequency. A data-re-uploading quantum circuit behaves differently: its
output is a Fourier series whose frequency set is fixed, exactly, by its encoding. We
join the two facts into a constructive design rule, Spectrum-Matched Circuit Design
(SMCD): read the frequencies a PDE's solution needs from the operator's Fourier symbol
and the forcing, then build the circuit whose frequency set covers them, with no
architecture search. The hybrid model mixes $K$ copies of the designed circuit through a
linear head. Six explainable-AI instruments, run identically on matched classical and
hybrid models, test the mechanism rather than only the accuracy, and every claim is
pre-registered with a numeric threshold and scored by a script. The circuit produces
exactly its designed frequencies (leakage below $4\times10^{-15}$). Designed frequencies
beat random ones of the same size on the heat equation ($6.5\times$, one copy), on
Poisson ($236\times$, eight copies) and on a groundwater problem ($9.9\times$, eight
copies), on every seed. On waterlogging of farmland from leaking canals, the eight-copy
circuit reaches 0.09\% error, $18\times$ more accurate than a classical network of the
same size, and places the waterlogged stretch to within 1\,m. The instruments explain
why copies matter: in one circuit every Fourier amplitude depends on the same angles, so
the optimiser trades the slow wave for the fast one, and a single copy loses to classical
baselines on Poisson. Two results bound the claim: on Poisson a classical model given
the same frequencies is $3.3\times$ more accurate than the eight-copy circuit, and at
high Helmholtz wavenumbers no model, classical or quantum, learns the solution.
\end{abstract}
