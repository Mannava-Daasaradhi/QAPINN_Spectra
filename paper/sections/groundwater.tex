\section{Application: waterlogging under canal-irrigated farmland}
\label{sec:groundwater}

SMCD needs a solution's frequencies before training. Real problems where those are known
in advance come from regular structure: evenly spaced sources, periodic geometry. This
section applies the method to one such problem, with its claims pre-registered like all
others (Section~\ref{sec:claims}).

\subsection{The problem}
Two rivers $L=2$\,km apart bound a confined aquifer beneath irrigated farmland. Water
enters from background recharge $R_0$ (rain and irrigation return flow) and from six
canals running parallel to the rivers at equal spacing, each leaking $q$ m$^3$/day per
metre of its length into a strip of width $\sigma$. Dupuit's steady one-dimensional
equation for the hydraulic head $h$ (water-table height above the river datum) is
\begin{equation}
  T\,h''(X) = -R(X),\qquad
  R(X) = R_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{6}\frac{q}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-(X-c_j)^2/2\sigma^2},
  \qquad h(0)=h_A,\ h(L)=h_B,
\end{equation}
with $c_j=(j-\tfrac12)L/6$. The scenario uses values within textbook ranges
\citep{freeze1979groundwater,todd2005groundwater}, not calibrated to one site:
transmissivity $T=100$\,m$^2$/day (silty sand), $R_0=0.4$\,mm/day, $q=0.4$\,m$^2$/day,
$\sigma=30$\,m, $h_A=1$\,m, $h_B=0$. On $x=X/L$ this is $-u''=f$, the Poisson operator
with non-zero boundary values, so every model and instrument applies unchanged. The
canal spacing fixes the frequencies, harmonics of $2\pi\cdot6$ in $x$, and SMCD designs
a one-qubit, four-layer circuit for them.

Because $\sigma(z\Phi(z)+\phi(z))$ with $z=(X-c)/\sigma$ has second derivative
$\phi(z)/\sigma$, the head has a closed form. An independent second-order
finite-difference solver agrees with it to $5\times10^{-8}$\,m and converges to it at
measured order $2.00$ as the grid is refined from 100 to 6{,}400 intervals
(Figure~\ref{fig:refconv}), which checks the closed form, the forcing and the boundary
treatment together.

\subsection{The practical question}
A field is waterlogged where the water table rises within 1.5\,m of the land surface,
into the crop root zone. With the surface 9\,m above the river datum, the water table
peaks at 8.53\,m and 713\,m of the 2\,km field is waterlogged
(Figure~\ref{fig:gwprofile}). The solution answers the planning questions a farmer or
irrigation engineer would ask (Figure~\ref{fig:gwscenarios}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Lining the canals.} Cutting canal seepage by 20\% dries the whole field;
    a 10\% cut reduces the waterlogged length from 713\,m to 494\,m.
  \item \textbf{Soil.} The same canals waterlog 1{,}778\,m on clay loam
    ($T=25$\,m$^2$/day) and none of the field on fine sand ($T\ge150$).
  \item \textbf{Spacing.} Concentrating the same total seepage into two canals
    waterlogs 1{,}066\,m and raises 84\,cm mounds under each canal; spreading it over
    twelve reduces the mounds to 1\,cm.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Can the trained models answer it?}
Five claims were pre-registered for the SMCD circuit and tested on held-out seeds
10--14 at the full training budget, with one copy and with eight, each against baselines
of its own size (Table~\ref{tab:gw}).

\paragraph{Eight copies meet all five claims.} The median error is 0.09\%, with every
seed below 0.5\%. That is $18\times$ more accurate than an MLP with the same 91
parameters, $9.9\times$ more accurate than the same circuit with random frequencies (both
on every paired seed), and more accurate than the 8{,}513-parameter MLP. 99.4\% of its
improvement over the MLP lies inside $\Omega$, and it places the waterlogged length at
714\,m against the exact 713\,m (Figure~\ref{fig:gwmodels}). Unlike on Poisson, the
classical model handed the same frequencies does not match it here (22.9\% error, no
waterlogging predicted). A plausible reason, not tested here, is that the solution's broad
mound is poorly represented by a linear head on fixed sine features, while the circuit's
trainable encoder can adjust its frequencies.

\paragraph{One copy meets one of five.} Its improvement over the MLP still lies on the
designed frequencies (99.4\%), but it does not learn the water table: its median profile
peaks near 4\,m instead of 8.5\,m, so it predicts no waterlogging
(Figure~\ref{fig:gwmodelsone}). A classical network of its size (13 parameters) reaches
5.0\% and the random-frequency circuit 6.0\%. This is the Poisson trade-off of
Section~\ref{sec:copyresult} again: the solution is dominated by a broad mound, the slow
component a single circuit gives up to fit the canal ripple.
