\section{Completing the pre-registered protocol}
\label{sec:fullprotocol}

The results above use the runs judged at the WISER 2026 challenge, which the deadline
cut to two seeds, one qubit count for PR-10 and a reduced training budget for PR-9.
After the challenge we ran the protocol as \texttt{docs/predictions.md} states it and
re-adjudicated with the same, unchanged checks
(\texttt{scripts/adjudicate\_predictions.py --protocol full}; record in
\texttt{results/adjudication\_v1\_full.json}). No prediction, threshold or check was
changed. Three configurations restore the plan:
\texttt{core\_matrix\_full} (five seeds, 210 runs: 176 had finished before the deadline
cut and were on disk, 34 were trained after the challenge),
\texttt{depth\_sweep\_full} (PR-10 at 4 and 6 qubits, three seeds; 8 qubits does not fit
in this machine's memory) and \texttt{coverage\_sweep\_full} (PR-9 at the full
20{,}000+2{,}000 budget). Runs already on disk were reused by their content hash, so
seeds 0--1 are the submitted runs themselves.

\paragraph{What changed.} Three checks move to CONFIRMED and none moves the other way.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PR-6 on heat} (design vs.\ random circuit): 6.5$\times$ lower error,
    five of five seeds agreeing, which is exactly what the pre-registered significance
    gate needs. \S\ref{sec:validation} shows the result survives bootstrap resampling
    and removal of any one seed. This is the design rule's main positive result: the
    same circuit with SMCD's frequencies beats it with random ones.
  \item \textbf{PR-4} (the negative control: nothing beats \texttt{c\_mlp} on heat by
    more than 5\%): at two seeds \texttt{q\_serial} appeared 7.7\% better; at five,
    every family is worse than \texttt{c\_mlp}, \texttt{q\_serial} by 38\%. The
    control holds, and it is a statement that the circuit does \emph{not} help on a
    smoothing operator.
  \item \textbf{PR-10} (barren plateaus): with two qubit counts the fit is defined. Its
    slope, $b=0.31$, is below the pre-registered $\log2=0.69$, so the verdict is
    CONFIRMED; but the fit explains almost none of the variation ($R^2=0.001$). The
    honest reading is that gradient variance shows no measurable decay between 4 and 6
    qubits, not that it decays slowly.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What did not change.} PR-1, PR-2 and PR-3 stay REFUTED with five seeds, and
more decisively (\S\ref{sec:validation}). PR-6 on Poisson stays INCONCLUSIVE (1.84$\times$,
but the seeds disagree). PR-8 on Poisson stays CONFIRMED (99.9\% inside $\Omega$). PR-9
stays REFUTED and becomes clearer: at the full budget the Poisson correlation is
$\rho=+0.29$, the wrong sign, against $-0.82$ at the reduced budget. PR-11 stays REFUTED
and PR-12 CONFIRMED (encoder drift 0.13 over five seeds). PR-7 on Poisson remains
unmeasurable, and cannot be measured by any run: the check splits the NTK spectrum at
eigen-index $|\Omega|=81$, and \texttt{q\_serial} has 14 parameters, so its NTK has at
most 14 non-zero eigenvalues.
