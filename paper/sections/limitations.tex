\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Simulation only, small circuits.} Every circuit runs on a statevector
  simulator; nothing here ran on quantum hardware, and noise is modelled by analytic
  surrogates checked against a density-matrix simulation. Each circuit is one qubit per
  copy without entanglement, which a classical computer evaluates cheaply, and simulation
  cost capped the experiments at 6--8 qubits. No claim of quantum computational advantage
  is made or tested.
\item \textbf{Scope.} The evidence covers one-dimensional problems and one applied
  scenario, with values from textbook ranges rather than a calibrated site.
\item \textbf{Statistical power.} Five seeds give a smallest Wilcoxon $p$ of 0.0625, so a
  confirmed comparison means all five seeds agree; bootstrap intervals and alternative
  tests support the central result but do not raise the sample size.
\item \textbf{Tuning.} The eight-copy model was selected from a small grid of copy counts
  and learning rates, while classical families received a learning-rate search only.
\item \textbf{The affine encoder.} The encoder is restricted to an affine map so that
  $\Omega$ is well defined; a nonlinear encoder would make the frequency set
  input-dependent.
\end{enumerate}
