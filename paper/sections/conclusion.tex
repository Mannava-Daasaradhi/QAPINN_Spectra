\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Against the contributions claimed in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, adjudicated strictly
against the pre-registered falsifiers of \texttt{docs/predictions.md} (12 predictions,
15 checks counting per-problem splits; \S\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:negative}):

\textbf{C1 (SMCD's constructive map) is not supported at the achieved sample size, with
one qualified exception at high wavenumber.} Its own falsifier, PR-6, is INCONCLUSIVE
on both checked problems --- $n=2$ seeds cannot statistically separate \texttt{q\_serial}
from \texttt{q\_random}, even where the raw ratio points the predicted way (heat,
$12.2\times$). The direct comparison against classical baselines on P1 (PR-1/PR-2/PR-3)
shows \texttt{q\_serial} \emph{underperforming} \texttt{c\_mlp}, \texttt{c\_ff}, and
\texttt{c\_rff\_matched} alike. Generalizing PR-3 to all six instances (\S\ref{sec:results})
sharpens this: REFUTED (classical decisively wins) on four of six, but genuinely
CONFIRMED --- matched-capacity statistical equivalence --- on the two highest-wavenumber
instances, Helmholtz\_k10 and Helmholtz\_k20, consistent with PR-5's $k$-scaling trend
(though there every family, classical and quantum, scores rel-$L^2\approx1$:
\S\ref{sec:errata}, E5).
Read together, the honest statement is not ``SMCD doesn't work'' but ``this run finds no
evidence of a quantum \emph{advantage} anywhere, and finds the quantum
\emph{disadvantage} narrowing to statistical parity specifically at the hardest,
highest-wavenumber instances'' --- a real, qualified finding, not the clean constructive
win C1 was written to claim.

\textbf{C2 (quantum-NTK block decomposition, flattened decay inside $\Omega$) is
reversed where measurable.} PR-7 REFUTED on Helmholtz\_k10: the quantum block's
spectrum was steeper, not flatter, inside the encoded band on this data. Poisson is
INSUFFICIENT\_DATA, not REFUTED --- fewer than 2 positive eigenvalues fall in the
outside-band index range of this run's committed NTK spectrum, so the comparison this
prediction needs is undefined there, reported as such rather than guessed at.

\textbf{C3 (spectral-bias relief as mechanism) holds on one instance, not the other.}
PR-8 CONFIRMED on Poisson (99.9\% of the real, positive improvement lands inside
$\Omega$; 71.3\% in the submitted version, \S\ref{sec:errata}) but REFUTED on Helmholtz\_k10, where \texttt{q\_serial} does not beat
\texttt{c\_mlp} at any frequency at all --- there is no improvement for the mechanism
to explain on the harder instance.

\textbf{C4 (the negative-result map) is right about the mechanism, wrong about the
uniform threshold.} Three of four quantum families tested on P2 (heat) underperform
\texttt{c\_mlp} exactly as the smoothing-operator argument predicts, \texttt{q\_random}
by an order of magnitude; \texttt{q\_serial}'s own modest $+7.7\%$ is the one quantum
counter-example, and two classical-family violations (\texttt{c\_ff},
\texttt{c\_rff\_matched}) are unrelated to the quantum-specific claim the 5\% bar was
meant to test. The mechanistic story --- smoothing operators give a high-frequency-rich
circuit nothing to earn its keep on --- is better supported by the per-family breakdown
than by the single uniform threshold PR-4 was pre-registered against.

\textbf{C5 (the XAI protocol) is the contribution this run actually demonstrates
cleanly.} Every number above is a falsifiable, mechanically-checked quantity, run
identically across classical and quantum families, computed from committed artifacts
by \texttt{scripts/adjudicate\_predictions.py} rather than asserted --- and the protocol
caught real bugs in its own supporting tooling along the way (a NaN silently
miscategorized as REFUTED in PR-8's early implementation, a degenerate least-squares fit
that would have reported a fabricated PR-10 slope, corrupted checkpoint data recovered
rather than silently used), which is itself evidence the falsification discipline this
paper argues for is not decorative.

\textbf{The honest headline, matching Section~\ref{sec:intro}'s own framing:} at the
sample size this deadline allowed ($n=2$, not the pre-registered $n=5$), this run does
not demonstrate the quantum-advantage hypothesis SMCD was built to test --- on the one
problem with a full ablation (Poisson), the hybrid model underperforms every classical
comparator, and the NTK-block and spectral-flattening mechanisms proposed to explain
\emph{why} it should help (C2) point the opposite direction from predicted. What does
hold up is the negative-result mechanism on the smoothing control (C4, mostly), one
genuine per-frequency win (C3, Poisson only), and the measurement protocol itself (C5)
--- which is precisely the outcome this project's own pre-registration discipline exists
to make legible rather than obscure. A larger, unrushed replication at the originally
planned $n=5$ seeds, and a \texttt{depth\_sweep} grid that retains $n_{\text{qubits}}$
variation (needed to even attempt PR-10), are the two most direct next steps
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
