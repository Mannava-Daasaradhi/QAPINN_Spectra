\section{Follow-up study (v2): pre-registered, in progress}
\label{sec:followup}

The diagnostics of \S\ref{sec:diagnostics} suggest specific changes to the quantum
model. Testing them honestly needs the same discipline as v1: tune on old data, commit
predictions, then test on runs that did not exist when the predictions were written.
The study is designed and partly run; its re-test is not finished, and this paper
reports none of its results as findings.

\paragraph{Design} (\texttt{docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-28-*-design.md}, committed
before any run). The v2 quantum model keeps SMCD's circuit and adds $K$ copies of it
(same encoding scalings, hence the same $\Omega$, each with its own angles) mixed by a
linear head, so the head gets independent handles on the Fourier amplitudes that v1's
single readout couples. The boundary ansatz becomes a tuned choice for every family,
between v1's mask ansatz and the affine ansatz of \S\ref{sec:diagnostics}. Every
classical family receives the same number of tuning configurations as the quantum
model. Tuning uses only v1's Poisson problem with seeds 0--2; the re-test uses seeds
10--14 and re-adjudicates v1's twelve claims, word for word, with the unchanged script.
The selection rule, including a parsimony rule on $K$, was committed before the first
lab result (\texttt{scripts/select\_v2\_config.py}).

\paragraph{What the lab has shown so far} (exploratory, not a finding). At the full
training budget on Poisson, two copies lower \texttt{q\_serial}'s median rel-$L^2$ from
1.63 to 1.27, and the best classical configuration reaches 0.35. A learning rate of
$10^{-2}$ makes the single circuit worse (3.55). The affine-ansatz arm and the larger
$K$ are still running. We state now, as the design did, that the v2 model may not beat
the classical baselines; the re-test will report whatever it finds.
