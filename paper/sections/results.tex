\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Errors are relative $L^2$ against the reference solution, reported as the median over
five seeds. A relative error of 1 is what the trivial prediction $u\equiv0$ scores, so
the scale runs from 1 (nothing learned) towards 0. The subsections take the evidence one
question at a time and say what each result means for the method.

\subsection{The circuit builds exactly what SMCD designs}
\label{sec:realise}
For every one-dimensional problem the SMCD circuit was evaluated at 40 random parameter
settings and its output Fourier-transformed. The largest amplitude outside the designed
set $\Omega$, relative to the largest overall, is at most $3.8\times10^{-15}$, which is
floating-point zero, and the test suite checks the same property on every build. The
frequency set could still drift during training, because the encoder in front of the
circuit is trainable; it does not. The encoder drift $\|A-I\|_F$ at the end of training
is 0.13 with one copy and 0.10 with eight, below the pre-registered 0.2 at which the
design card would stop describing the trained model.

\emph{What this means.} Proposition~1 and the ternary depth rule are not only theory: the
design card computed before training is a correct, checkable description of the model
after training. Every later statement about ``inside'' and ``outside'' $\Omega$ rests on
this.

\subsection{Designed frequencies beat random ones}
\label{sec:design}
The sharpest test of SMCD changes only one thing: the circuit, its size and its training
stay fixed, and the encoding scalings are either SMCD's or random draws from $U(0.5,3)$.
A worked example shows what is at stake. With four layers, random scalings of at most 3
reach frequencies of at most $4\times3=12$ radians per unit, while the heat and Poisson
solutions contain a $15\pi\approx47$ wave; SMCD's scalings $\pi\cdot\{1,3,9,27\}$ reach
$40\pi\approx126$. The random circuit cannot represent the fast wave at any parameter
setting. The design wins on every seed of every comparison:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Heat, one copy:} $6.5\times$ lower error on all five seeds
    (Figure~\ref{fig:designrandom}). The designed circuit is also the most consistent
    model in the study, its worst and best seeds within 4\% of each other, while its
    random twin varies by $7.4\times$.
  \item \textbf{Poisson, eight copies:} $236\times$ (0.0037 against 0.87), all five
    held-out seeds.
  \item \textbf{Groundwater, eight copies:} $9.9\times$ (0.09\% against 0.92\%), all five
    held-out seeds (Section~\ref{sec:groundwater}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{How robust is the heat result?} Resampling the five paired seeds 5{,}000 times
gives a 95\% bootstrap interval of 2.5$\times$ to 17.7$\times$ for the error ratio, entirely
above the pre-registered 1.5$\times$ bar. Removing any one seed leaves the ratio above the
bar all five times, so no single seed carries the result. With five pairs the smallest
possible Wilcoxon $p$ is 0.0625 however large the effect; tests without that floor make
the strength visible: a paired $t$-test on log error gives $p=0.006$ and a Mann--Whitney
test $p=0.008$.

\emph{What this means.} This is the central claim of the method, and it holds on every
problem where it was tested: choosing the encoding from the PDE's symbol is worth one to
two orders of magnitude in accuracy over the same circuit with an arbitrary encoding, at
no cost in parameters.

\subsection{The mechanism in the frequency domain}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\paragraph{The improvement lands on the designed frequencies.} Projecting the error onto
Fourier modes at every checkpoint and accumulating how much better the circuit does than
the MLP at each frequency, 99.9\% of the circuit's total improvement on Poisson lies
inside $\Omega$ with one copy, 99.9\% with eight, and 99.4\% on groundwater; the
pre-registered bar was 70\%. Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} shows it directly: the MLP's error
at high $|\omega|$ persists through training (the spectral-bias staircase), while the
circuit clears the rows marked by $\Omega$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/freq_heatmap_poisson.pdf}
  \caption{Poisson: per-frequency error $|\hat e(\omega)|$ over training, MLP (left,
    \texttt{c\_mlp}) against the SMCD circuit (right, \texttt{q\_serial}), seed 0, with
    $\Omega$ overlaid as thin white lines.}
  \label{fig:heatmaps}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The NTK is flatter inside $\Omega$.} Proposition~4 says the quantum block's
kernel keeps non-decaying eigen-directions at the encoded frequencies, so its spectrum
should be flatter inside $\Omega$ than outside, the opposite of a classical network's
power-law decay. With eight copies on Poisson the decay exponent inside $\Omega$ is
flatter than outside by 15.2, against a pre-registered bar of 0.5.

\emph{What this means.} Both instruments see the same thing from two sides: the circuit
learns fast exactly where the design puts its frequencies. The accuracy gains come from
the mechanism SMCD is built on, not from an incidental property such as size or
initialisation.

\paragraph{SMCD also says when not to use a circuit.} On the heat equation diffusion
damps each Fourier mode at a rate proportional to $\omega^2$, so the $15\pi$ ripple in the
initial condition vanishes almost immediately. SMCD's design card, computed before any
training, predicts a benefit of only 2.5\% from a quantum layer, and the pre-registered
check confirms it: no model improves on a plain MLP by more than 5\%. A user learns this
from the equation alone, before spending any compute.

\paragraph{No barren plateau at this scale.} Gradient variance fitted against qubit count
(4 and 6 qubits, three seeds) has slope 0.31, under the pre-registered $\log2=0.69$ bar:
no decay is detectable between 4 and 6 qubits. SMCD's shallow depths, local observable
and small initial angles are designed to avoid the plateau, and at this scale they do.

\subsection{Circuit copies}
\label{sec:copyresult}

\paragraph{What one circuit cannot do.} The Poisson solution is
$\sin\pi x+0.3\sin15\pi x$. Tracking the error at those two frequencies
(Figure~\ref{fig:specerr}), a single circuit cuts its $15\pi$ error to a median 10\% of its
starting value by step 1{,}000, faster than any classical model in the study (Fourier
features 103\%, MLP 86\%). Its $\pi$ error falls to about 30\% by step 5{,}000 and then
\emph{rises} to about twice its starting value by step 20{,}000, on all five seeds.

A worked number explains the trade. The PDE residual contains $u''$, and differentiating
$\sin\omega x$ twice multiplies it by $\omega^2$, so an error at $15\pi$ costs $15^2=225$
times as much loss as the same error at $\pi$. All 81 Fourier amplitudes of a single
circuit are functions of the same ten angles, read out through one expectation value, so
moving the angles to fix the $15\pi$ amplitude also moves the $\pi$ amplitude, and the
optimiser accepts that because the loss it saves at $15\pi$ is 225 times larger. The loss
stalls near $3.3\times10^4$ from step 1{,}000 while the error keeps moving, and all five
seeds converge to the same trade-off.

  \caption{Poisson, one copy: error spectrum at each checkpoint (seed 0), with the error at

\paragraph{Copies decouple the amplitudes.} With $K$ copies the head has $K$ independent
weights on $K$ independently parametrised circuits, all with the same $\Omega$
(Section~\ref{sec:copies}). On the tuning seeds the error falls with the number of copies
(Figure~\ref{fig:copies}); at the better of the two learning rates it is 1.67 with one
copy, 1.27 with two, 0.08 with four and 0.003 with eight, almost three orders of magnitude.

\paragraph{Eight copies on held-out seeds.} On seeds 10--14, which played no part in
choosing the model, the eight-copy circuit reaches a median error of 0.0037, with every
seed below 0.02. Five pre-registered claims hold on all five seeds: it beats the
Fourier-feature network by three orders of magnitude and the same circuit with random
frequencies by $236\times$; 99.9\% of its improvement over the MLP lies inside $\Omega$;
its NTK is flatter inside $\Omega$ by 15.2; and its encoder drifts by only 0.10. Every
circuit seed trained stably at the selected learning rate.
