\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Full derivations for every item below are in \texttt{docs/derivations/}; this section
states results and the one-line reason each matters to the design rule in
Section~\ref{sec:methodology}.

\subsection{Classical side}

\textbf{PINNs as strong-form collocation.} A PINN \citep{raissi2019pinn} minimizes the PDE residual directly at
sampled collocation points via automatic differentiation, with no mesh and no weak form
--- unlike Galerkin/FEM, the optimization problem is non-convex in the network parameters
even when the underlying PDE is linear.

\textbf{Exact derivatives via reverse-mode AD.} Reverse-mode AD gives derivatives that are
exact (to floating-point precision), not finite-difference approximations; the dominant
cost for a PINN is second derivatives (e.g.\ $u_{xx}$ in $\ge 2$ dimensions), computed via
double backward passes.

\textbf{The NTK and lazy training} \citep{jacot2018ntk} \textbf{(Section~\ref{sec:prop4}'s classical baseline).} Writing
$\Theta(x,x')=\nabla_\theta N[u_\theta](x)\cdot\nabla_\theta N[u_\theta](x')^\top$ for the
residual network's tangent kernel, gradient-flow training linearizes in the lazy regime to
$\dot e = -\Theta_0 e$, so each eigenmode of $\Theta_0$ decays as $\exp(-\lambda_i t)$:
loss-term imbalance between residual and boundary blocks is an eigenvalue-imbalance
phenomenon, not merely a loss-weight-tuning problem (\texttt{docs/derivations/03\_ntk\_pinn.md}).

\textbf{Spectral bias.} For a tanh or ReLU MLP, the NTK eigenvalues decay roughly as a
power law in Fourier-mode index, so high-frequency solution components are learned last
and slowly (Figure~\ref{fig:staircase}, measured on the Poisson problem studied here).
Fourier-feature and random-feature encodings \citep{tancik2020fourier} are the standard
classical fix and a necessary baseline (Section~\ref{sec:setup}): if the quantum layer
beats only a plain MLP and not a Fourier-feature network, it has bought nothing that
Fourier features do not already supply.

\textbf{Hard vs.\ soft boundary enforcement.} The ansatz $u = B(x) + D(x)\cdot N(x)$
(distance function $D$ vanishing on the boundary, particular solution $B$ matching the
boundary data) removes the boundary-loss NTK block entirely by construction, at the cost
of a distance-function derivation per domain; the soft-BC alternative keeps a
separately-weighted boundary loss term whose own NTK block this project instruments
directly (Section~\ref{sec:xai}).

\subsection{Quantum side}

\textbf{Data-re-uploading circuits as Fourier series.} For encoding gates
$\exp(-ix H^{(l)})$ with generator eigenvalues $\{\lambda^{(l)}_a\}$, an $L$-layer
re-uploading circuit's output expectation is a truncated Fourier series whose frequency
set is the set of signed sums of per-layer eigenvalue differences --- the central theorem
this project's entire methodology is engineering on top of (Proposition~1,
Section~\ref{sec:prop1}; \citealp{schuld2021effect}).

\textbf{Parameter-shift derivatives.} $\partial_\theta f = \tfrac12[f(\theta+\pi/2) -
f(\theta-\pi/2)]$ for a Pauli-generated gate; because the circuit's input $x$ enters as a
rotation angle, $\partial_x f$ is \emph{also} exactly computable via a parameter shift ---
a hardware-exact PINN residual gradient, not a finite-difference approximation, and the
reason this project's whole approach is physically realizable on real quantum hardware,
not just simulable.

\textbf{Barren plateaus.} $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_\theta C]$ decays exponentially in qubit
count $n$ for a global-observable cost function on a deep, unstructured random circuit
\citep{mcclean2018barren}. SMCD's Algorithm~1 (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}) mitigates this
structurally: shallow depth (by design, not by luck --- the depth rule targets the
\emph{minimum} $L$ that covers the target band), a local observable, and small-angle
initialization.

\textbf{Effective dimension.} The Abbas et al.\ effective-dimension statistic
\citep{abbas2021effdim}, computed from the empirical Fisher information eigenspectrum, is
this project's quantum-native capacity measure (Section~\ref{sec:xai}'s Fisher/effective-dimension
instrument), reported alongside --- not instead of --- parameter count, since ``fewer
parameters'' is a claim prior QPINN work makes loosely.
