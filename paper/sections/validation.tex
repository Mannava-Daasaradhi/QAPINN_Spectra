\section{Robustness and validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Every verdict above comes from one pre-registered rule applied to five seeds. This
section asks how much those verdicts depend on that rule, on individual seeds, and on
the tools that produce the numbers. All of it is computed from committed runs
(\texttt{scripts/report/fig\_validation.py}, record in
\texttt{results/report/validation.json}); no model was retrained.

\paragraph{Bootstrap intervals.} Figure~\ref{fig:bootstrap} resamples the five paired
seeds 5{,}000 times and gives a 95\% interval for each pre-registered error ratio. The
heat ablation (PR-6) is the robust positive result: the SMCD circuit's error is 6.5$\times$
lower than the same-size random circuit's, with the whole interval (2.5$\times$ to
17.7$\times$) above the 1.5$\times$ bar. The Poisson claims fail decisively rather than
narrowly: PR-1's interval (0.40 to 1.10) never reaches its 2$\times$ bar, PR-2's (0.01 to
0.43) lies entirely on the wrong side of 1, and PR-3's (3.9 to 5.8) lies entirely outside
its equivalence band. PR-6 on Poisson (1.84, interval 0.97 to 7.2) straddles its
threshold, which is what INCONCLUSIVE should look like.

\paragraph{Other tests.} With five paired seeds the smallest two-sided Wilcoxon $p$ is
$1/16=0.0625$, however large the effect, so the pre-registered gate ($p\le0.0625$) can
only be met by five out of five seeds agreeing. Figure~\ref{fig:alttests} applies three
alternatives to the same pairs. They agree with every verdict, and they show what the
Wilcoxon floor hides: on the heat ablation a paired $t$-test on log error gives
$p=0.006$ and a Mann--Whitney test $p=0.008$.

\paragraph{Leave one seed out.} If one seed decided a verdict, dropping it would flip
the ratio across its threshold. Figure~\ref{fig:loso} repeats each ratio five times with
one seed removed. PR-6 on heat clears its bar in all five, PR-1 to PR-3 miss theirs in
all five, and PR-6 on Poisson clears it in four of five: its INCONCLUSIVE verdict reflects
disagreement across seeds, not one outlier.

\paragraph{The circuit does what the design says.} For every one-dimensional problem,
the SMCD circuit was evaluated at 40 random parameter settings and its output
Fourier-transformed. The largest amplitude outside the designed set $\Omega$, relative to
the largest overall, is at most $3.8\times10^{-15}$, i.e.\ floating-point zero
(Figure~\ref{fig:circuitspec}). This repeats \texttt{tests/test\_circuit\_spectrum.py}
across problems and random draws: the design rule builds exactly the frequency set it
claims. (The two-dimensional Helmholtz designs are not covered by this one-dimensional
check.)

\paragraph{The reference solver.} The groundwater problem of \S\ref{sec:groundwater} is
scored against a closed-form solution. A second-order finite-difference solver,
independent of that formula, converges to it at measured order $2.00$ as the grid is
refined from 100 to 6{,}400 intervals (Figure~\ref{fig:refconv}), which checks the
closed form, the forcing and the boundary treatment together.
