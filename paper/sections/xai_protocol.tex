\section{Measurement protocol}
\label{sec:xai}

\subsection{Explainable-AI instruments}
Six instruments run at the same checkpoints ($\{0,100,500,1\text{k},5\text{k},
20\text{k},\text{final}\}$ steps) for every model, problem and seed, on matched classical
and hybrid families, so a measured difference cannot come from when or how each family
was probed. This addresses the post-hoc storytelling that PINN explainability work is
criticised for \citep{fundamental_flaws_pinns}: each instrument's expected signature is
stated as a claim before the runs (Section~\ref{sec:claims}).

\paragraph{NTK spectroscopy.} Following \citet{ntk_pikans_2506} for classical
physics-informed solvers, extended here to the hybrid case: the empirical NTK on a fixed
probe set, its eigenvalue spectrum, decay exponent, condition number and effective rank.
The expected signature is a flatter spectrum inside the encoded band $\Omega$ than
outside it (Proposition~4). NTK drift $\|\Theta_t-\Theta_0\|_F/\|\Theta_0\|_F$ tracks
whether a model stays in the lazy-training regime.

\paragraph{Per-frequency error trajectory.} The error field $u_\theta - u^*$ projected
onto Fourier modes at each checkpoint, shown as a frequency-by-step map with $\Omega$
overlaid. A classical PINN gives the spectral-bias staircase; if the hybrid model's
improvement falls inside $\Omega$, the mechanism is visible directly rather than inferred
from an aggregate error.

\paragraph{Collocation-point attribution.} Integrated Gradients of the residual loss with
respect to the input coordinates, with the completeness violation and the correlation
between attribution and true residual reported alongside the field, since Integrated
Gradients is known to be unstable.

\paragraph{Fisher information and effective dimension.} The empirical Fisher spectrum and
the effective dimension of \citet{abbas2021effdim}, tracked through training for every
family, to separate ``more expressive'' from ``more parameters''.

\paragraph{Gradient variance.} $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_\theta L]$ against qubit count and
depth, compared with the $2^{-n}$ decay expected for a global cost on a deep random
circuit \citep{mcclean2018barren}.

\paragraph{Layerwise probes and loss landscapes.} Ridge-regression $R^2$ per layer for
how much of $u^*$ is linearly decodable there, and filter-normalised two-dimensional
loss-landscape slices around the trained point.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Problems.} Six one- and two-dimensional instances on the unit domain, all
with exact or high-accuracy references: Poisson, $-u''=f$ with solution
$\sin\pi x+0.3\sin15\pi x$, a slow and a fast wave; the heat equation with a
$15\pi$ ripple in the initial condition, a smoothing operator; viscous Burgers,
nonlinear; and two-dimensional Helmholtz at $k=4,10,20$. A seventh, groundwater flow
under irrigated farmland, is the application of Section~\ref{sec:groundwater}.

\paragraph{Model families.} Every family uses the same hard boundary ansatz and training
loop. The SMCD circuit (one copy, or $K$ copies, Section~\ref{sec:copies}); the same
circuit with random encoding scalings drawn from $U(0.5,3)$, the size-matched test of the
design itself; a parallel hybrid (an MLP beside the circuit) and an octave-split ensemble
(Section~\ref{sec:octave}); an MLP (8{,}513 parameters on Poisson), a Fourier-feature
network (129) and a frequency-matched classical model, a linear head on sine and cosine
features at exactly the frequencies SMCD targets, sized to match the circuit. SMCD builds
one-qubit, four-layer circuits for Poisson, heat and Burgers ($|\Omega|=81$, 14--18
parameters) and three-qubit, one-layer circuits with a ring of CZ gates for Helmholtz
($|\Omega|=15$). Only the frequency-matched model and the random-frequency circuit are
size-matched to the circuit; the MLP and Fourier-feature network run at their standard
sizes. On the groundwater problem an MLP of the circuit's size is added.

\paragraph{Training.} 20{,}000 Adam steps followed by 2{,}000 L-BFGS steps, five seeds per
configuration. The one-copy circuit and all six problems use seeds 0--4 at a common
learning rate. The eight-copy circuit was selected on the Poisson problem with seeds 0--2
from eight configurations ($K\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, two learning rates), while each classical
family received eight learning-rate candidates, under a selection rule fixed before any
tuning run; all of its reported results are on held-out seeds 10--14, which played no
part in selection. Circuits are simulated exactly by a statevector simulator
cross-checked against PennyLane to $10^{-10}$.

\subsection{Pre-registered claims and how they are scored}
\label{sec:claims}
Every claim about the method was written with a deciding metric and a numeric threshold
in \texttt{docs/predictions*.md} and committed to git before the runs that test it;
\texttt{scripts/verify\_preregistration.py} checks that the committing revision is an
ancestor of every such run. Claims about the eight-copy circuit were written after model
selection on the tuning seeds and before any held-out run. A script
(\texttt{scripts/adjudicate\_predictions.py}, \texttt{adjudicate\_groundwater.py}) issues
every verdict from the committed run records. A claim is \emph{confirmed} when its
threshold is met and, for a comparison between two models, all five paired seeds agree
(paired Wilcoxon $p\le0.0625$, the smallest value five pairs allow); \emph{refuted} when
the threshold is missed; \emph{inconclusive} when the median meets the threshold but the
seeds disagree. No threshold is changed after a run.
