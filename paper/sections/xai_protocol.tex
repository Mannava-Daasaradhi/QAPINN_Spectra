\section{Measurement protocol}
\label{sec:xai}

\subsection{Explainable-AI instruments}
Six instruments run at the same checkpoints (0, 100, 500, 1k, 5k, 20k steps and the final
step) for every model, problem and seed, so a measured difference cannot come from when or
how each family was probed (Table~\ref{tab:instruments}). Each instrument's expected
signature is stated as a pre-registered claim, which guards against the post-hoc
storytelling PINN explainability work is criticised for \citep{fundamental_flaws_pinns}.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Problems.} Three problems on the unit domain, each with an exact reference:
Poisson, $-u''=f$ with solution $\sin\pi x+0.3\sin15\pi x$, a slow and a fast wave, the
case spectral bias makes hard; the heat equation with a $15\pi$ ripple in the initial
condition, a smoothing operator on which SMCD predicts little benefit; and groundwater
flow under irrigated farmland, the application of Section~\ref{sec:groundwater}.

\paragraph{Model families.} Every family uses the same hard boundary ansatz and training
loop. The SMCD circuit (one copy, or $K$ copies, Section~\ref{sec:copies}); the same
circuit with random encoding scalings drawn from $U(0.5,3)$, the size-matched test of the
design itself; an MLP (8{,}513 parameters) and an MLP of the circuit's size; a
Fourier-feature network (129); and a frequency-matched classical model, a linear head on
sine and cosine features at exactly the frequencies SMCD targets, sized to match the
circuit. For all three problems SMCD builds a one-qubit, four-layer circuit with
$|\Omega|=81$ frequencies (14--18 parameters per copy).

\paragraph{Training.} 20{,}000 Adam steps followed by 2{,}000 L-BFGS steps, five seeds per
configuration. The one-copy circuit uses seeds 0--4 at a common learning rate. The eight-copy circuit was selected on the Poisson problem with seeds 0--2
from eight configurations ($K\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, two learning rates), while each classical
family received eight learning-rate candidates, under a selection rule fixed before any
tuning run; all of its reported results are on held-out seeds 10--14, which played no
part in selection. Circuits are simulated exactly by a statevector simulator
cross-checked against PennyLane to $10^{-10}$.

\subsection{Pre-registered claims and how they are scored}
\label{sec:claims}
Every claim about the method was written with a deciding metric and a numeric threshold
in the repository's \texttt{docs/} folder and committed to git before the runs that test
it; a verification script checks that the committing revision is an ancestor of every
such run. Claims about the eight-copy circuit were written after model selection on the
tuning seeds and before any held-out run. An adjudication script issues every verdict
from the committed run records. A claim is confirmed only when its threshold is met and,
for a comparison between two models, all five paired seeds agree (paired Wilcoxon
$p\le0.0625$, the smallest value five pairs allow). No threshold is changed after a run.
The complete set of pre-registered claims and their verdicts is published with the code.

\subsection{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}
Everything above is implemented in one open codebase (about 5{,}400 lines of library
code, 4{,}500 of experiment and analysis scripts and 6{,}300 of tests). Its parts are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A statevector simulator} for data-re-uploading circuits with exact
    derivatives in the input and the angles, cross-checked against PennyLane to
    $10^{-10}$ and against a density-matrix simulation for the noise surrogates.
  \item \textbf{SMCD itself}: the symbol-based target spectrum, the depth and width
    rules, the octave split and the design card, with a test that every designed circuit
    emits nothing outside $\Omega$.
  \item \textbf{One training harness} for all seven families (hard or soft boundary
    conditions, Adam then L-BFGS, deterministic seeding) that records the six instruments
    at fixed checkpoints and stores every run under a content hash of its configuration.
  \item \textbf{An adjudication pipeline} that reads only the stored run records, issues
    every verdict, verifies that each claim was committed before its runs, and redraws
    every figure and table without retraining.
\end{itemize}
The experiments comprise 327 recorded training runs, about 130 hours of training on one
laptop GPU. 402 automated tests and a continuous-integration job rerun the test suite,
re-derive every verdict and compare every figure against the committed record on each
change.
